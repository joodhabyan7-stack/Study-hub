📕 Eleven — Sandra Cisneros

Study

Eleven is a story about growing up and how, even when someone gets older, they can still sometimes feel like a younger version of themselves.

Important Ideas

  • Growing up
  • Identity
  • Embarrassment
  • Standing up for yourself
  • Different ages and emotions

Vocabulary

Identity

The qualities and characteristics that make someone who they are.

Embarrassed

Feeling uncomfortable or ashamed because of something that happened.

Flashcards

What is one major theme?

Growing up and understanding yourself.

What can the number eleven represent?

Being between childhood and becoming older.

🏀 The Game

Background

Basketball is a fast-moving game with two main objectives: scoring by getting the ball into the other team's hoop and stopping the other team from scoring.

Up to five players from each team can be on the court. Baskets can be worth 1, 2, or 3 points, and the team with the most points wins.

The story follows an important school basketball game.

Page 1

The team has practiced extensively and feels ready for the championship. A huge crowd arrives, but the team has not seen the opposing team play before.

The opposing team gets the first tap and moves the ball extremely quickly. They score early while the narrator's team struggles to respond.

The score quickly becomes 6–0 and then 8–0. The opposing team appears to be enjoying themselves.

Page 2

After another mistake and a foul, Mr. Reese calls a timeout. He tells the players to stay calm, take their time and trade baskets instead of rushing.

He uses hand signals to communicate with the players. Chalky, Sam and Leon work together to create an opportunity for Clyde to score the team's first points.

Vocabulary: Rebound

A rebound happens when the ball bounces back after a missed shot.

Page 3

The opposing team's quick passing continues, but the narrator's team begins to defend better.

Chalky blocks an outside shot and the team gets a chance to score on a fast break.

Mr. Reese eventually puts the narrator into the game. The narrator is nervous but remembers the coach's advice: when you don't know what to do, keep moving.

Page 4

The narrator's movement helps create an easy scoring opportunity. Later, the narrator catches the ball near the corner and makes an important shot.

The opposing team begins paying special attention to the narrator. The narrator scores again with a lay-up.

The team's defense also improves as Chalky, Sam and Leon work together to control the defensive backboard.

Mood Change

At first, the team is nervous and discouraged. As they begin playing better and scoring, they become more confident and hopeful.

Page 5

The score is 36–29, with the opposing team ahead by seven points. Mr. Reese remains calm and tells the players to stay cool.

The team follows his signals and slowly gets closer to the opposing team.

With six minutes remaining, Mr. Reese puts the narrator and Turk into the game while taking out two of the team's strongest players.

Turk unexpectedly makes an important lay-up. The team continues to work together, and Leon completes a three-point play.

Mr. Reese then tells the team to use a strategy they had practiced: intentionally putting pressure on the opposing player so that the player might rush a shot.

Page 6

The story explains the fouling strategy. A player who is fouled while shooting can receive two foul shots, while a player fouled when not shooting receives one.

The narrator runs toward an opponent as if he is going to foul, but stops before making contact. The opponent becomes rushed and throws the ball wildly.

The ball goes over the baseline, giving the narrator's team possession.

Mr. Reese then removes the narrator and Turk and brings Sam and Chalky back into the game.

Page 7

The game is almost over. The team realizes that the score became tied while the narrator and Turk were playing.

Sam and Chalky help the team outscore the opponent during the final minutes.

The team wins first-place trophies.

Mr. Reese explains that he is proud of them not only because they won, but because they worked hard together.

Everyone applauds. The narrator cries, and Leon cries even more.

Vocabulary: Boo-hooing

Another way of describing crying.

⭐ Main Theme

One of the main ideas of the story is that teamwork, trust, preparation and staying calm can be just as important as having the strongest individual players.

🧠 Quick Review

  • Who is the coach? Mr. Reese
  • What sport is being played? Basketball
  • What does rebound mean? The ball bouncing back after a missed shot
  • How does the team's mood change? From nervous and discouraged to confident and hopeful
  • What helps the team succeed? Teamwork, preparation, strategy and trust

➗ Math

Fractions & Decimals

Multiplying Decimals

Multiply the numbers normally, then place the decimal point based on the total number of decimal places in the factors.

Adding Fractions

When fractions have the same denominator, add their numerators and keep the denominator.

Subtracting Fractions

Subtract the numerators when the denominators are the same.

Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

Dividing Fractions

Keep the first fraction, change division to multiplication, and use the reciprocal of the second fraction.

⚗️ Chemistry

Particles

Everything around us is made of tiny particles. These particles are far too small to see with our eyes.

Elements

An element is a substance made from one type of atom. Elements have chemical symbols.

Fe

Iron

Na

Sodium

Cl

Chlorine

Compounds

A compound is a substance made when two or more different elements are chemically joined together.

For example, sodium and chlorine can combine to form sodium chloride, commonly known as table salt.

Mass

Mass tells us how much matter is in an object.

Volume

Volume tells us how much space something takes up.

Density

Density describes how much mass is packed into a certain volume.

Density Formula

Density = Mass ÷ Volume
