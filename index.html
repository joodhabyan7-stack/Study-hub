📚 Eleven — Study

👧 Rachel

Rachel is turning eleven. She is excited about being eleven, but she still sometimes feels like a much younger child.

🎯 Main Idea

Growing older does not mean that all your younger feelings and experiences disappear.

💡 Even when you get older, your past experiences can still be a part of you.

🔴 The Red Sweater

The red sweater is important because Rachel's teacher incorrectly believes that it belongs to Rachel.

This makes Rachel feel embarrassed, frustrated, and misunderstood.

✍️ Figurative Language

Rachel uses comparisons to describe how huge and overwhelming the sweater feels to her.

📐 Fractions & Decimals

Practice multiplying, dividing, adding, and subtracting fractions and decimals.

📚 Math — Study

✖️ Multiplying Decimals

When multiplying decimals:

  1. Multiply the numbers as if they were whole numbers.
  2. Count all the decimal places in the original numbers.
  3. Put that many decimal places into your answer.
2.4 × 1.3 = 3.12

✖️ Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the top numbers together, then multiply the bottom numbers together.

2/3 × 4/5 = 8/15

➗ Dividing Fractions

A common method is:

KEEP → CHANGE → FLIP
2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 5/6

➕ Adding Fractions

If the denominators are the same, add the numerators. If the denominators are different, find a common denominator.

1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4

➖ Subtracting Fractions

Just like addition, fractions with different denominators need a common denominator.

5/6 − 2/6 = 1/2

🧪 Chemistry — Learn the Basics

1. 🔵 What Are Particles?

A particle is a very tiny piece of matter. Scientists use the word "particle" to talk about the tiny pieces that substances are made from.

These particles are far too small to see with your normal eyes.

Example:

A piece of iron might look like one solid object to us, but it contains an enormous number of tiny particles.

💡 Think of particles as tiny building blocks that make up the materials around you.

2. ⚪ What Is an Atom?

An atom is a tiny building block of an element.

Different elements are made from different types of atoms.

Example:
  • Iron is made of iron atoms.
  • Sodium is made of sodium atoms.
  • Chlorine is made of chlorine atoms.
⭐ Remember:

Element = one type of atom.

3. 🧪 What Is an Element?

An element is a pure substance made from only one type of atom.

Scientists have identified many different elements. Each element has its own name and chemical symbol.

Some examples:
  • Fe = Iron
  • Na = Sodium
  • Cl = Chlorine
  • O = Oxygen
  • H = Hydrogen
  • C = Carbon

4. 🔤 Chemical Symbols

Chemical symbols are short ways of writing the names of elements.

A symbol can have one or two letters. The first letter is capitalized. If there is a second letter, it is lowercase.

Na means sodium.

Cl means chlorine.

Fe means iron.

💡 Some symbols don't look exactly like the English name. For example, Na comes from the Latin name for sodium.

5. 🧩 What Is a Compound?

This is an important word!

A compound is a substance made when atoms of two or more different elements chemically join together.

Example: Table Salt 🧂

Sodium is represented by Na.

Chlorine is represented by Cl.

When they chemically combine, they form sodium chloride (NaCl).

Sodium + Chlorine → Sodium Chloride
💡 A compound is not simply two substances sitting next to each other. The elements are chemically joined.

6. ⚖️ What Is Mass?

Mass tells us how much matter is in an object.

Mass is commonly measured in:

  • grams (g)
  • kilograms (kg)

Scientists can use a balance to measure mass.

Example:

If a small object has a mass of 50 g, that tells us how much matter is in that object.

7. 📦 What Is Volume?

Volume tells us how much space something takes up.

For liquids, volume is often measured in:

  • milliliters (mL)
  • liters (L)

The volume of some solid objects can be measured in cubic centimeters (cm³).

Example:

A bottle containing 500 mL of water has a liquid volume of 500 mL.

8. 🧱 What Is Density?

Density tells us how much mass is packed into a certain amount of space.

Imagine two boxes that are exactly the same size. If one box contains much more mass than the other, the first box has a greater density.

Density = Mass ÷ Volume
Example:

An object has a mass of 20 g and a volume of 5 cm³.

Density = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 g/cm³

🧠 Easy way to remember:

More mass in the same space = greater density.

9. 📏 Measuring Volume

A graduated cylinder can be used to measure the volume of a liquid.

For an irregular solid object, scientists can use water displacement.

Water displacement:

Put water into a graduated cylinder and record the starting volume. Carefully place the object into the water. The water level rises. The increase in volume tells you the volume of the object.

⭐ Chemistry Quick Review

  • Particle: a tiny piece that makes up matter.
  • Atom: a tiny building block of an element.
  • Element: a pure substance made of one type of atom.
  • Compound: a substance made when different elements chemically join.
  • Mass: the amount of matter in an object.
  • Volume: the amount of space something takes up.
  • Density: mass packed into a certain volume.
