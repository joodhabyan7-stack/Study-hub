🏀 The Game

A basketball story about teamwork, practice, confidence and winning together.

🏀 The Game — Study

📚 Background

Basketball is a fast-moving team sport. Teams try to score points by getting the ball through the other team's hoop while also preventing the other team from scoring.

Key facts: Teams can have up to five players on the court at one time. A basket can be worth one, two, or three points depending on the shot.

📄 Page 1

The team has practiced a lot and feels ready for the championship. However, once the game begins, the opposing team immediately shows how strong they are at passing and scoring.

  • The opposing team gets the first tap.
  • They pass the ball extremely quickly.
  • They quickly take an 8–0 lead.
  • The narrator's team struggles with shooting and possession.
  • Sam walks while carrying the ball.
Figurative language: “We had practiced and practiced until it ran out of our ears” means they had practiced an enormous amount.

📄 Page 2

Chalky fouls an opponent, so Mr. Reese calls a timeout. He tells the players not to rush and gives them signals for when he wants them to make a move.

  • Chalky, Sam and Leon set picks.
  • They pass to Clyde and score their first two points.
  • The opposing team begins to lose some confidence.
  • The opposing team's supporters cheer with yellow flags and sweaters.
  • Leon misses a hook shot and the other team gets the rebound.
Rebound: When the ball comes back after a missed shot and a player gets possession of it.

📄 Page 3

The narrator's team begins figuring out how to defend against the other team's quick passing. Chalky blocks a shot and the team scores.

Mr. Reese puts the narrator into the game. The narrator is extremely nervous, but remembers his coach's advice: if you don't know what to do, keep moving.

  • Chalky blocks a shot.
  • Sam helps move the ball forward.
  • The narrator enters the game.
  • The narrator passes to Leon.
  • The narrator keeps moving and begins creating a play with Sam.

📄 Page 4

The narrator catches the ball near the corner and shoots. He remembers Mr. Reese's advice about following his shot, and the ball goes straight into the basket.

The opposing team starts warning their players to watch number 17 — the narrator.

The team begins making a comeback. Chalky and Sam defend strongly, while the team controls the defensive rebounds.

Mood change: The team moves from nervous and discouraged to confident and hopeful.

📄 Page 5

The score is 36–29, with the opposing team ahead by seven points. Mr. Reese remains calm and tells everyone to keep playing patiently.

  • The team comes within one point.
  • The opposing team increases its lead to five.
  • With six minutes left, Mr. Reese puts the narrator and Turk in.
  • Sam and Chalky, two strong players, are taken out.
  • Turk unexpectedly makes a lay-up.
  • Leon scores while being fouled, creating a three-point play.
  • Mr. Reese tells the team to foul the opponent.
Strategy: Mr. Reese trusts the plan they practiced instead of simply relying on the team's strongest players.

📄 Page 6

The narrator explains the team's fouling strategy. The plan is to pressure the opposing player into taking a rushed shot.

  • A shooting foul can result in two foul shots.
  • A foul when the player isn't shooting can result in one foul shot.
  • The team hopes the opponent will rush a shot when expecting a foul.
The narrator is trusted to help carry out the strategy.

📄 Page 7 — Ending

The narrator runs toward an opposing player as if he is going to foul him. At the last moment, he stops without touching him. The opponent throws the ball wildly, and it goes out of bounds.

Mr. Reese then puts Sam and Chalky back into the game. The narrator and Turk discover that the score had become tied while they were playing.

During the final four minutes, Sam and Chalky help the team outscore the opponents by four points.

🏆 The team wins the championship!

Mr. Reese gives a speech about how proud he is of the team, especially because they worked hard together. The narrator cries from happiness, and Leon cries even more.

⭐ Main Theme

The Game shows how teamwork, practice, trust, determination and hard work can help a group succeed.

The victory isn't just about one player. Everyone contributes to the team's success.

➗ Fractions & Decimals — Study

Multiplying Decimals

  1. Multiply the numbers as if they were whole numbers.
  2. Count the decimal places in both numbers.
  3. Put the decimal point into the answer.
2.4 × 1.3 = 3.12

Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators.

2/3 × 4/5 = 8/15

Dividing Fractions

Use Keep, Change, Flip.

2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 5/6

Adding Fractions

Find a common denominator when necessary, then add the numerators.

1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4

Subtracting Fractions

5/6 − 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2

🧪 Chemistry

🧪 Chemistry — Study

1. Particles

Particles are extremely tiny pieces that make up matter.

2. Atoms

Atoms are tiny building blocks that make up elements.

3. Elements

An element is a pure substance made from one type of atom.

Examples: Iron (Fe), Sodium (Na), Chlorine (Cl), Oxygen (O)

4. Chemical Symbols

Element Symbol
Iron Fe
Sodium Na
Chlorine Cl
Oxygen O
Hydrogen H
Carbon C

5. Compounds

A compound is a substance made when atoms of two or more different elements chemically join together.

Sodium + Chlorine → Sodium chloride (NaCl)

Sodium chloride is the chemical name for table salt.

6. Mass

Mass is the amount of matter in something. It is commonly measured in grams (g) or kilograms (kg).

7. Volume

Volume is the amount of space something takes up.

  • Liquids can be measured in mL or L.
  • Solids can be measured in cm³.

8. Density

Density tells us how much mass is packed into a certain volume.

Density = Mass ÷ Volume

Example: If an object has a mass of 20 g and a volume of 5 cm³:

20 ÷ 5 = 4 g/cm³

9. Measuring Volume

A graduated cylinder can be used to measure the volume of liquids.

For an irregular solid, water displacement can be used to find its volume.
