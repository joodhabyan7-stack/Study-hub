📚 Study: Eleven

⭐ Main Idea

Rachel is turning eleven, but she realizes that growing older does not make her forget the younger versions of herself. Her past experiences are still part of who she is.

👧 Rachel

Rachel is the main character. She feels embarrassed and frustrated when her teacher wrongly says that a red sweater belongs to her.

👕 The Red Sweater

The sweater represents Rachel's embarrassment and frustration. She knows it is not hers, but she feels powerless to speak up.

💭 Important Idea

Rachel learns that growing older does not mean you suddenly feel completely grown-up. Sometimes you can still feel like your younger self.

✨ Figurative Language

The story uses comparisons to describe the sweater and Rachel's feelings.

  • The sweater is compared to a big red mountain.
  • The sweater is compared to a waterfall.
  • Rachel's younger ages represent the experiences that remain part of her as she grows.

📚 Math Study

1️⃣ Multiplying Decimals

Multiply the numbers as if there are no decimal points. Then count the total number of decimal places in the original numbers and put the decimal point into the answer.

Example:
2.4 × 1.3 = 3.12

2️⃣ Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominators together.

Example:
2/3 × 4/5 = 8/15
💡 Simplify your answer if possible.

3️⃣ Dividing Fractions

Use Keep, Change, Flip.

  • Keep the first fraction.
  • Change division to multiplication.
  • Flip the second fraction.
Example:
2/3 ÷ 4/5
= 2/3 × 5/4
= 10/12
= 5/6

4️⃣ Adding Fractions

If the denominators are the same, add the numerators and keep the denominator.

Example:
2/7 + 3/7 = 5/7

If the denominators are different, find a common denominator first.

Example:
1/2 + 1/4 = 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4

5️⃣ Subtracting Fractions

Make sure the fractions have a common denominator, then subtract the numerators.

Example:
5/6 − 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2
Another example:
3/4 − 1/2
= 3/4 − 2/4
= 1/4

⭐ Remember

  • Always simplify fractions when possible.
  • For addition and subtraction, denominators need to match.
  • For multiplication, multiply straight across.
  • For division, Keep → Change → Flip.
  • For decimals, carefully count decimal places.
